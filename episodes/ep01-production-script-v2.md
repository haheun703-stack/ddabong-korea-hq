# DDABONG KOREA — EP01 Production Script v2

Target length: ~7 minutes
Production rule: **Script-first, visual-aware**

## 0:00–0:35 — COLD OPEN

**VO** Walk through Gyeongju today, and something feels unusual.
- `[REAL]` Modern Gyeongju street, cafés, low buildings, people walking.

**VO** Between roads, houses, and everyday city life, enormous green mounds rise from the ground.
- `[REAL]` Slow reveal of Daereungwon/tumuli from street level or elevated view.

**VO** They look like hills.
- `[REAL]` Tight shot that hides scale, then widen.

**VO** But they are tombs.
- `[GRAPHIC]` Minimal on-screen text: `ROYAL TOMBS · SILLA`.

**VO** Some were built for the rulers and elite of Silla, the kingdom that made Gyeongju its capital for centuries.
- `[GRAPHIC]` Korea map → Gyeongju → Silla capital.

**VO** So here is the question: why would a society give this much land, labor, and wealth to the dead?
- `[REAL]` Hold on mound.
- `[SFX]` Music drops slightly before the question.

---

## 0:35–1:25 — FACT: WHAT IS ACTUALLY HERE?

**VO** Gyeongju is not a city with just a few old graves. Its historic landscape preserves palaces, temples, fortresses, and groups of royal tombs from Silla.
- `[REAL]` 3–4 quick shots: tombs / Wolseong area / temple heritage / city.
- `[GRAPHIC]` Simple layer map, not a dense tourist map.
- Source basis: UNESCO S1.

**VO** UNESCO describes the Tumuli Park area as three groups of royal tombs.
- `[GRAPHIC]` Highlight the three groups schematically.
- Source: S1.

**VO** Beneath many of these mounds were wooden burial spaces covered with stone and gravel.
- `[GRAPHIC]` Clean cross-section animation: coffin → wooden chamber → stone/gravel → earth mound.
- Source: S1/S3.

**VO** Excavations revealed gold, glass, fine ceramics, weapons, ornaments, and horse equipment.
- `[MUSEUM]` Rights-cleared object shots only.
- Source: S1/S3.

**VO** The mound is only the outside of the story. The real story is what people deliberately chose to place inside.
- `[MUSEUM]` Slow macro of one key object, preferably gold crown detail.

---

## 1:25–2:15 — CASE STUDY: CHEONMACHONG

**VO** One of the best-known examples is Cheonmachong — the Tomb of the Heavenly Horse.
- `[REAL]` Cheonmachong exterior/sign/entrance if filming is permitted.

**VO** It was excavated in 1973 as Tomb No. 155, during a test excavation before work on the much larger Hwangnamdaechong.
- `[ARCHIVE]` Rights-cleared 1973 excavation photo if available; otherwise `[GRAPHIC]` date + site plan.
- Source: S3.

**VO** More than eleven thousand objects were recovered, including a gold crown and a birch-bark saddle flap painted with a heavenly horse.
- `[MUSEUM]` Crown.
- `[MUSEUM]` Heavenly Horse painting image only if rights-cleared; otherwise licensed/public government source.
- Source: S3/S6.

**VO** That image gave the tomb the name we use today.
- `[GRAPHIC]` `Cheonma = Heavenly Horse`.

**VO** But the person buried here is still not known with certainty.
- `[GRAPHIC]` `OCCUPANT: UNCERTAIN`.
- Source: S4.

**VO** That uncertainty matters. History does not always give us a name — but the things people built and buried can still show us choices.
- `[REAL]` Quiet exterior shot.

---

## 2:15–3:15 — HOW A TOMB LIKE THIS WAS BUILT

**VO** Cheonmachong was not a simple grave dug into the ground.
- `[GRAPHIC]` Empty ground cross-section.

**VO** A wooden outer chamber was built. A coffin and grave goods were placed inside. Stone was piled above and around the chamber, and earth formed the giant mound.
- `[GRAPHIC]` Construction sequence based on museum dimensions/structure.
- Source: S3.

**VO** Now imagine the work behind that sequence.
- `[HIGGSFIELD]` Begin reconstruction. Label `AI VISUAL RECONSTRUCTION`.

**VO** Timber had to be prepared. Stone had to be carried. Valuable objects had to be selected. People had to coordinate the burial. And the mound itself had to be raised on a monumental scale.
- `[HIGGSFIELD]` Four short shots: timber preparation / stone transport / object preparation / coordinated mound work.
- Avoid fantasy armor, palace clichés, or invented royal face close-ups.

**VO** Whatever else this funeral meant, it required organization, resources, and many human decisions.
- `[HIGGSFIELD]` Wide shot of coordinated work, not melodramatic mourning.

---

## 3:15–4:20 — WHAT THE GOLD TELLS US

**VO** Then there is the gold.
- `[MUSEUM]` Gold crown reveal.

**VO** The Gyeongju National Museum describes the peak of Silla gold culture in the fifth to early sixth century as a time when the ruling elite built huge tombs in the royal capital and placed elaborate gold objects with the dead.
- `[MUSEUM]` Crown / belt / ornaments montage.
- Source: S2.

**VO** More importantly, the museum explains that gold was used to express sacredness, legitimacy, and enduring authority.
- `[GRAPHIC]` Three restrained words: `SACREDNESS · LEGITIMACY · AUTHORITY`.
- Source: S2.

**VO** That gives us firmer ground than simply guessing what people believed.
- `[REAL]` Return to tomb exterior.

**VO** We cannot read the exact thoughts of a Silla official fifteen centuries ago. But we can see that status and authority were deliberately made visible in elite burial culture.
- `[MUSEUM]` Detail shot of gold object → dark fade.

---

## 4:20–5:20 — MINDSET RECONSTRUCTION

**VO** So let us reconstruct the problem, not an invented conversation.
- `[GRAPHIC]` Small label: `INTERPRETATION`.

**VO** A ruler or powerful member of the elite dies.
- `[HIGGSFIELD]` Interior preparation scene, respectful and restrained. `AI VISUAL RECONSTRUCTION`.

**VO** The person is gone. But rank, legitimacy, relationships, and authority still matter to the people who remain.
- `[HIGGSFIELD]` Officials/artisans/family figures positioned by role; avoid explicit dialogue.

**VO** The burial now becomes a moment when those things can be made visible — through scale, ritual, precious materials, and the place chosen for the tomb.
- `[HIGGSFIELD]` Gold object prepared → procession detail → mound construction.

**VO** We do not have a Silla text saying, “Build this mound to prove that order continues.”
- `[GRAPHIC]` `NO DIRECT QUOTATION`.

**VO** But the archaeological evidence allows us to ask whether these monumental funerals also communicated status and authority to the living.
- `[HIGGSFIELD]` Final wide shot from viewpoint of observers watching the growing mound.

**VO** That is the difference between inventing their thoughts and trying to understand why their choices made sense.
- `[REAL]` Match cut from reconstructed mound to modern mound.

---

## 5:20–6:05 — THE CROWN CHANGES MEANING

**VO** Look again at the gold crown from Cheonmachong.
- `[MUSEUM]` Crown hero shot, rights-cleared.

**VO** Today it sits under museum lighting, separated from the place where it was buried.
- `[MUSEUM]` Display case / gallery context if filming rights allow.

**VO** But originally, this was not an object made for a museum. It belonged to a burial system of status, ceremony, and identity.
- `[GRAPHIC]` Tomb cross-section with crown position as a simplified illustration, only if archaeologically supported.

**VO** And that changes the question from “How beautiful is this?” to “Why did placing something this valuable with the dead make sense?”
- `[MUSEUM]` Slow detail.

**VO** That second question is where the story becomes human.
- `[REAL]` Visitor looking toward tomb/museum, no identifiable close-up needed.

---

## 6:05–6:40 — MODERN GYEONGJU

**VO** Today, people walk between these mounds. Families take photographs. Visitors step inside Cheonmachong. Cars and cafés sit only a short distance away.
- `[REAL]` Modern everyday montage.

**VO** What was once an elite burial landscape has become part of the everyday landscape of modern Gyeongju.
- `[REAL]` Wide public-space shot.

**VO** The past is not hidden outside the city. The city lives around it.
- `[REAL]` Best present/past contrast shot.

---

## 6:40–7:05 — CLOSING

**VO** So why are giant tombs everywhere in Gyeongju?
- `[REAL]` Return to opening mound composition.

**VO** Because these were never just piles of earth. They were built through labor, ritual, wealth, and deliberate social choices.
- `[HIGGSFIELD]` 2–3 second flashes of construction, then back to real footage.

**VO** We may never know every thought of the people who stood here fifteen centuries ago.
- `[REAL]` Quiet hold.

**VO** But the tombs show us what they chose to make visible: rank, legitimacy, memory, and authority.
- `[MUSEUM]` Crown detail / `[REAL]` mound.

**VO** And that is how we get closer to understanding not only what Silla built — but why building it this way may have made sense to them.
- `[REAL]` Sunset/wide closing shot.

**VO** DDABONG KOREA. Stories Behind Korea.
- `[GRAPHIC]` Channel end card.
